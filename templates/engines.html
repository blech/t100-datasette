Aircraft

Jets with two engines took over the long haul

In 1990 most flights of 3,000 miles or more were flown by aircraft with three or four jet engines, like Boeing's 747, the Douglas DC-8 and DC-10, and Lockheed's L-1011. Twin jets - planes with two engines - went from only 19% of those departures in 1990, up to 53% in 2001, and 88% in 2019. Tri-jets, the DC-10, MD-11 and L-1011, were almost gone by 2007, and four-engined jets fell from 55% to 5.5%.

Freight tells a slower version of the same story: jets with two engines carried 4% of long-haul freight in 1990 and 60% in 2025, while four-engined freighters, mostly 747s, still carry 55% of what dedicated freighters move.
